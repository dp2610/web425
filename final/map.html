Beginner Pickleball Guide

Rules and Scoring

Beginner importance: ★★★★☆

Serving starts every point, so it is one of the first rules beginners should learn. The serve must be hit underhand with the paddle moving upward, and the ball has to go diagonally into the opposite service box. If the serve lands in the kitchen or crosses the kitchen line, it is considered a fault, while all other lines are considered in bounds. The ball is still in play if it hits the net and lands in the correct service box. In doubles, the score uses three numbers: your team’s score, the opposing team’s score, and the server number. Only the serving team can score points during a rally. Usually, the player on the right side starts serving, and if the team wins the point, the server switches sides with their partner and continues serving. If they lose the point, the second teammate serves next, and once both players lose their serve, the serve switches to the other team.

Kitchen and Net Rules

Beginner importance: ★★★★★

The kitchen is the non-volley zone near the net, and it is one of the most important areas to understand in pickleball. You cannot stand in the kitchen and hit the ball out of the air, but you can enter it after the ball bounces. Near the net, beginners should focus on soft control shots instead of trying to smash everything. It is important to play smart rather than aggressive, meaning you should stay patient, strategic, and hit controlled low shots to avoid giving the opposing team easy points. Playing at the kitchen line also requires careful positioning, good timing, and quick reactions since you are so close to the net.

Positioning

Beginner importance: ★★★★☆

Where you stand changes the way you play. The serving line is better for fast reaction shots and returns, the center court area can help with controlled pushes, and the front kitchen line is useful for quick dinks and speed play. A strong beginner habit is moving with purpose instead of getting stuck in the middle. Playing closer to the kitchen requires quicker reactions because the ball travels much faster near the net and points can end quickly. On the other hand, playing farther back gives you more time to react and control your shots, but it can also make it harder to stay aggressive and take control of the point.

Paddles

Beginner importance: ★★☆☆☆

Paddles can change how much power, control, and spin a player gets. Wood paddles are usually cheaper but heavier, while composite and carbon fiber paddles often feel lighter and easier to control. For beginners, a balanced paddle is usually better than one that is only made for power. After learning the game you can experiment with different paddle types to find what works best for your style. Most people prefer moving to lightweight carbon fiber paddles since they offer a good balance of performance and comfort and are great for strong shots, controlled shots, spin, and movement.

Balls

Beginner importance: ★★☆☆☆

Pickleball balls may look simple, but indoor and outdoor balls can feel different. Outdoor balls are usually harder and have smaller and more holes so they can handle wind better and maximize speed on rough outdoor courts. While indoor balls are softer and have less bigger holes, making them easier to control and better bounce on smooth surfaces. Beginners should pay attention to the ball type based on if they are playing indoor or outdoor because it affects bounce, speed, and how rallies feel.
