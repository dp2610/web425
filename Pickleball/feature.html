A Day in my life of Playing Pickleball

Let me walk you through a full pickleball session from the planning stage to the end of the day. Instead of only explaining rules or showing equipment, let me show you how a normal pickleball run actually happens with friends so you can see if this is something you would enjoy.

Before the Game Starts

Most pickleball days start before we even get to the court. Usually, someone sends a message in the group chat asking who is down to play, and then everyone starts figuring out what time works. Since today we went as a group of four, it was perfect because that meant we could run doubles the whole time without waiting for extra players or have to look for other people at the courts with similar skill levels. Once the plan was set, I started getting ready by grabbing my paddle, water bottle, extra balls, a speaker and anything else I might need. This part feels small, but it actually matters because if someone forgets a paddle or balls because balls break and crack so we always need to carry extras.

Session mood: ★★☆☆☆

Highlight: The best part of this stage is the planning because this is when the session starts becoming real and everyone starts getting ready.

Getting There and Warming Up

The Walk to the Courts

The walk there is part of the day too because that is usually when everyone starts talking about teams, predictions, and who thinks they are about to win. Sometimes people are already making excuses before we even touch the court, saying they are tired or that they have not played in a while and that might affect their gameplay today. Once we get closer, the energy usually changes because everyone starts getting more serious and watching people playing when we get closer gets us more excited. We check if the courts are open, put our stuff down, and start getting ready to play.

Stretching and First Hits

Before starting games, we usually stretch a little and warm up with easy shots. We start with simple rallies just to get used to the ball and the court. After that, we do some dinks near the kitchen line because that is one of the hardest parts of pickleball to control. This warm up helps everyone get into rhythm before the games become locked in to the game and play more competitive during actual games.

Section rating: ★★★☆☆

Highlight: The warm up is important because this is where everyone shakes off mistakes and starts getting into rhythm before the games actually count.

The Games and Rotations

Starting the Series

Since there were four of us, we played doubles and switched teams so everyone could play with different partners. We start by randomizing teams through an online website to decide the first match up, and then run best of three or best of five series depending on how much time we have. Today, we played three full best of five series because we got here early and everyone wanted a fair chance to prove which team combination was actually the best. This made the games feel more serious because one lucky win was not enough.

Dinks, Rallies, and Mistakes

During the games, the rallies started getting longer because everyone adjusted after the first few points. Some points were won from fast shots at the baseline, while others came from soft dinks near the kitchen line. There were also plenty of mistakes, like hitting the ball into the net, missing easy returns because we try to avoid giving the ball back too high, or trying to smash when a soft shot would have been smarter. Those mistakes are part of what makes the session fun because everyone reacts, laughs, and then tries to fix it on the next point. Best movements were when someone pops the ball up high and the hitter tries to smash the ball down but missed the ball, or hits in the net, or hits it outside the lines.

Competition Builds Over Time

As the series went on, people started taking it more seriously. We began calling out strategies, aiming at weaker spots and weaker people, and trying to control the pace instead of just hitting randomly. The team at the kitchen usually had the advantage because they could react faster and place the ball better. By the last few games, every point felt more intense because everyone wanted to win the final series. Especially one of my friends that had lost the last two series and made it his mission to win the last one and redeem himself. That made the games more fun because there was a clear storyline and motivation for everyone to play their best.

Section rating: ★★★★☆

Highlight: This is the most competitive part of the day because teams keep changing, strategies start forming, and every series feels like it has a storyline.

After the Session

Cooling Down and Talking After

After the games ended, we stayed around for a little instead of leaving right away. We talked about the best points, the worst misses, and which teams worked best. Sometimes the after game conversation is just as fun as the actual games because everyone has a different memory of what happened or share different perspectives of certain movements. It also gave us time to relax, drink water, and talk about when we are playing again before having to walk back to the apartment.

What the Day Shows

A pickleball session is not just about the final score. It starts with making the plan, getting the gear, walking to the courts, warming up, playing full series, making mistakes, improving, and then talking about everything afterward. That full process is why pickleball became such a regular activity for us. It is easy to start, but every time we play, there is still something new to learn.

Section rating: ★★★★★

Highlight: The best part is that the session does not end right after the final point because the conversations after the games make it feel more social.

Why We Keep Playing

By the end of the session, the main thing I realized is that pickleball works because it gives everyone something different. Some people like the competition, some find it a great way to stay active and do cardio, and some just like being outside with friends. For us, it became a routine because it combines all of those things into one activity. The day starts with a simple group chat message, but it turns into a full experience with preparation, games, jokes, strategy, and improvement. Today's session doesn't show the perfect example of social play because we went a a group of 4 and chose to play within the group, but usually we also play with other people at the courts and meet new players which adds to the social aspect of the game. Usually, I will go to the courts alone or with one friend and then find other people to play with, which is a great way to meet new people and make friends through the game. I will either play singles or doubles depending on how many people are there and what the skill levels are, but I always find it fun to play with new people and see how different people approach the game. That is one of the things that makes pickleball so fun and addictive, there is always something new to learn and new people to meet through the game and is a great way to socialize and connect with new people.
